Meet JACKPOT

What happens from the moment a swab is collected to the moment a public health alert goes out — and the software that makes it possible.

JACKPOT stands for Just Another Collection of Killer Pathogen ‘Omics Tools. It's an open-source platform that any public health agency, academic lab, or research consortium can deploy to track infectious disease outbreaks using genomic sequencing.
🦠

What it tracks

Viruses, bacteria, and other pathogens — from COVID-19 and flu to Salmonella and Valley fever

🧬

How it works

By reading the genome of each pathogen, it can tell whether two cases are related or coincidental

🏥

Who uses it

Public health officials, academic researchers, and epidemiologists tracking disease spread across the host jurisdiction and beyond

Why Sequence the Genome at All?

Traditional lab tests tell you what pathogen is present. Genomic sequencing tells you which exact version — and that difference is huge for outbreak detection.

The postal code analogy: Knowing someone has COVID is like knowing they live in "California." Knowing their lineage is like knowing their exact street address. It lets you trace who infected whom.
1

Cluster Detection

When two patients have genomes that differ by only 1–2 mutations, they likely share a transmission chain. JACKPOT makes this comparison automatic.

2

Variant Tracking

Which new variants are circulating in your jurisdiction? When did they arrive? Is this a local spread or travel-imported? The genome answers all of these.

3

National Reporting

JACKPOT formats genomic data for NCBI, GISAID, and CDC — so local data feeds into the global picture.
